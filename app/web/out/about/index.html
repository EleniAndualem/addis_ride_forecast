◎Overview

The business problem

Ride demand in Addis Ababa swings by zone, hour, weather and events. Too few drivers means lost rides; too many means idle cars. Operators need a dependable hourly forecast before the demand arrives.

Objective

  • 1Forecast hourly trips for 12 zones, 1–14 Nov 2025
  • 2Beat simple rules using only forecast-time inputs
  • 3Turn forecasts into drivers, fares and a range of outcomes

How to use the dashboard

  • • Pick a day in the strip and a zone on the map or list.
  • • Drag or play the hour slider to watch demand move across the city.
  • • Weather and events are looked up for you; ⚡ marks a zone with an event that day.
  • • Drivers needed = forecast ÷ 1.3 trips per driver-hour.

▤Data

Four input tables

🚕Trips (training)
85,460 rows · 1 Jan – 31 Oct 2025

Hourly trips per zone, plus fares, waits and active drivers (history only, never model inputs).

🗓️Forecast grid
4,032 rows · 1 – 14 Nov 2025

12 zones × 24 hours × 14 days. We predict trips for each.

🌦️Weather
7,538 rows · Hourly

Temperature, rain, humidity, wind. Stored in UTC; shifted +3 h to Addis time.

🎟️Events calendar
165 events · 8 types

Football, concerts, conferences, holidays, road closures; messy names and dates.

⇢Pipeline

Six phases

  1. 1
    Clean

    28 data problems logged and fixed: ×8 export spikes, −9999 rain codes, a Fahrenheit block, duplicate hours.

  2. 2
    Integrate

    Weather and events joined to every zone-hour (100% match) after the UTC → EAT clock fix.

  3. 3
    Explore

    14 analysis questions answered with numbers: trend, rhythm, rain, events, holidays, data quality.

  4. 4
    Engineer

    36 features known at forecast time: calendar, zone, weather, events, demand history.

  5. 5
    Model

    10 approaches compared on chronological splits; LightGBM (Poisson) tuned on earlier folds only.

  6. 6
    Deliver

    4,032 forecasts with 80% / 90% ranges, this dashboard, a report and slides.

Final model

LightGBM (Poisson) on 36 features, trained 1 Jan – 31 Oct 2025. A Poisson objective suits counts whose spread grows with their level. Ranges come from calibrated residuals.

△Limitations & next steps

Limitations

Holiday eves

9 of the 10 biggest misses fall on the eve of a holiday the calendar does not list.

Surges are under-called

The model catches 57% of peak hours; alerting on the top of the 80% range catches 92%.

Synthetic, short history

Ten months of generated data; Ayat only opened on 15 March.

Inputs taken as given

Uses the provided weather forecast; driver supply and pricing are not modelled.

Next steps

  • • A religious-calendar feed, so holiday eves are known in advance
  • • Per-zone uncertainty instead of one pooled range width
  • • Weekly retraining and live error monitoring
